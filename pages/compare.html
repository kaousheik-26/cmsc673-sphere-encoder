Guessing a video from its first frame: FrameNet vs RotNet

Both models are shown only the first frame of a video and a moment in time, and must guess where that later frame sits on the image encoder's sphere. Every picture is a point on that sphere, so a video is a path. FrameNet guesses the whole jump in one go. RotNet splits it into how far the video moves and which way.

Fair match: both have 10 M parameters, both see the same 24 training clips and the same 16 new clips, and both trained for 4000 steps.

Say it out loud: Both models learn how far a video moves, but on new videos neither can tell which way it moves. Hand RotNet the right direction and its error drops from 20° to 4.5°.

Idea A. FrameNet: one jump

  • In: the first frame's point on the sphere, and a time (0 to 5 s).
  • Out: one arrow. Follow it along the sphere and you land on the guess.
  • Learns from: how far the guess lands from the true frame.
  • Distance and direction are tangled together in that one arrow.

Idea B. RotNet: how far, then which way

  • In: the same first frame and time. One version also sees frame 2 as a hint.
  • Out: two answers. How far: one angle in degrees (the dashed ring). Which way: a direction (the arrow).
  • Learns from: the error on each answer separately.
  • So we can check, and swap out, each half on its own.

How they work: architecture and losses

Both models share the same body (a small transformer that reads the first frame and the time) and differ only in the head (what they output) and the loss (how they are graded). Read top to bottom.

1
Input: the first frame's latent \(z_0\). The frozen sphere encoder turns a 256×256 image into 256 tokens × 384 numbers (98,304 numbers in all). All of them together lie on a sphere of radius √98,304 ≈ 313.5, so every picture is one point on that sphere.
2
Time \(t=\text{frame}/80\), from 0 to 1. It goes through a sine/cosine code (like a clock face at many speeds), then a 2-layer MLP, giving a 384-number time vector c.
3
Body: 4 transformer blocks, width 384, 6 heads. Each token is first projected (384→384) and gets a learned position vector. Inside every block, the time vector c sets a scale and shift on each LayerNorm (adaptive LayerNorm: "the same network, tuned to this moment in time"), then self-attention lets the 256 tokens look at each other, then an MLP (384→1536→384) works on each token. The scale/shift layers start at zero, so the body starts as a plain transformer.

FrameNet head and loss

4
One arrow. A linear layer turns each token into 384 numbers: an arrow \(\xi\) with the same shape as \(z_0\). The part that points straight out of the sphere is removed, so the arrow lies flat on the sphere's surface at \(z_0\).
5
Walk along the sphere (the "exp map"): start at \(z_0\), walk along the surface in the arrow's direction for the arrow's length.
$$\hat z_t=\cos\theta\,z_0+\sqrt{L}\,\sin\theta\,\frac{\xi}{\lVert\xi\rVert},\qquad \theta=\frac{\lVert\xi\rVert}{\sqrt{L}},\quad L=98{,}304$$
At t = 0 the arrow is forced to zero, so frame 0 is copied exactly.
L
Loss: one minus the cosine between guess and truth.
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{FrameNet}}=1-\cos(\hat z_t,\,z_t)$$
It is 0 when the guess is exactly right. Distance and direction mistakes are paid in the same currency, so the model never has to say which one it got wrong.

10.15 M parameters.

RotNet head and loss

4a
Which way: a linear layer per token gives an arrow. It is laid flat on the sphere like FrameNet's, then scaled to length 1, so it is a pure direction \(\hat d\) and carries no distance.
4b
How far: average the 256 tokens, attach the time vector c, then a small MLP (768→384→1). A softplus keeps it positive and ×10 sets the scale, giving one angle \(\hat\theta\) in degrees. It is forced to 0 at t = 0.
5
Rotate \(z_0\) by \(\hat\theta\) degrees toward \(\hat d\). This is the same walk along the sphere as FrameNet's, with the length and the direction given separately.
$$\hat z_t=\cos\hat\theta\,z_0+\sqrt{L}\,\sin\hat\theta\,\hat d$$
L
Loss: first split the truth the same way. The "log map" finds the arrow that walks from \(z_0\) to \(z_t\); its length is the true angle \(\theta^*\) and its unit direction is \(d^*\). Then grade each half separately:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{RotNet}}=\underbrace{\frac{|\hat\theta-\theta^*|}{10}}_{\text{how far}}+\underbrace{\big(1-\cos(\hat d,\,d^*)\big)}_{\text{which way}}$$
Exchange rate: 1° of distance error costs 0.1, the same as pointing 26° the wrong way.

10.44 M parameters (the extra 0.3 M is the "how far" MLP). One variant also feeds the step from frame 0 to frame 1 as a hint (the "frame-2 hint").

Training, both models: pick 64 random (clip, frame) pairs from the 24 training clips, predict each frame from frame 0 and its time, apply the loss, AdamW step (learning rate 3e-4, cosine decay, gradient clip 1.0). 4000 steps for the results below; the live run continues the same recipe to 200k.

Analogy: directions to a house

FrameNet says "go to that spot." RotNet says "walk 2 km, heading north-east." Both models learn the 2 km part well. For a house they have never visited, neither can guess the heading. So they walk the right distance in the wrong direction, and end up further away than if they had stayed home.

Where the error comes from: how far vs which way

Read it like this. The how far columns are small for every model: distance is easy, carries over to new videos, and RotNet is best at it. The which way column is the problem. On new clips every model is about 82° off, close to a blind guess, and a model 10 times bigger does not change that. Swap in the true direction and the pictures land close to the best the encoder can redraw (LPIPS ).

What this means

  1. Splitting the answer works for distance. RotNet gets "how far" within on training clips and on new ones (FrameNet: and ).
  2. Direction is the whole problem. On new clips both models are about 82° off. Overall it is close to a tie (RotNet , FrameNet , RotNet better on of 16 clips), and both are worse than freezing ().
  3. The split shows exactly what to fix. With the true direction, RotNet drops to and its pictures beat freeze on of 16 new clips. FrameNet can't run that test because its two halves are tangled.
  4. Bigger isn't the fix. The 106 M FrameNet memorises the training directions ( off) but not new ones (). A frame-2 hint doesn't help either. We need more clips, or a direction signal taken from motion.

Setup: 24 training and 16 new 5 s clips (81 frames at 16 fps) from one text-to-video model, encoded by the sphere image encoder. Errors are averaged over every frame after the first. 10 M models: width 384, 4 layers, batch 64, learning rate 3e-4, 4000 steps. 106 M FrameNet: width 768, 11 layers, 25k steps. FrameNet has no direction output, so its "how far" and "which way" are recovered from three angles (start to true, start to guess, guess to true) with the spherical law of cosines. Angle numbers come from the path-map runs and can differ from the training summaries by up to 0.2°.
