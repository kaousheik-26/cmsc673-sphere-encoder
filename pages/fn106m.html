FrameNet at 106 M parameters: 10 times bigger, same answer

FrameNet is shown only the first frame of a video and a time, and guesses where that later frame sits on the image encoder's sphere (every picture is a point on it; a video is a path). We grew it from 10 M to 106 M parameters to test whether the small model was simply too small.

Say it out loud: A model 10 times bigger memorises the training videos almost perfectly, but it is no better on new ones. Size is not what is holding it back; the data is.

Architecture of the 106 M model

Input: first frame's latent \(z_0\)
frozen sphere encoder, 256 tokens × 384 numbers, on a sphere of radius √98,304 ≈ 313.5
↓
Linear 384 → 768 per token, + learned position
0.30 M + 0.20 M parameters
↓
11 transformer blocks, width 768, 12 attention heads
each block: adaptive LayerNorm (scale and shift from time) → self-attention over the 256 tokens → adaptive LayerNorm → MLP 768 → 3072 → 768. 9.45 M parameters per block (attention 2.36 M, MLP 4.72 M, time modulation 2.36 M).
↓
Head: Linear 768 → 384 per token gives an arrow \(\xi\) the shape of \(z_0\)
0.30 M parameters, starts at zero so the first guess is "stay put"
↓
Lay the arrow flat on the sphere, then walk along it (exp map)
$$\hat z_t=\cos\theta\,z_0+\sqrt{L}\,\sin\theta\,\frac{\xi}{\lVert\xi\rVert},\quad \theta=\frac{\lVert\xi\rVert}{\sqrt L}$$ At t = 0 the arrow is zeroed, so frame 0 comes back exactly.
Time \(t=\text{frame}/80\) ∈ [0, 1]
sine/cosine code at many speeds → MLP 768 → 768 → 768 = time vector \(c\) (1.18 M parameters). \(c\) sets the scale and shift inside every one of the 11 blocks.

10 M vs 106 M, side by side

Learning rate lowered and warm-up lengthened for the larger model, the usual recipe for stable training at this size. Both models see the same 24 training clips and batches of 64 (clip, frame) pairs.

The objective: one number per guess

$$\mathcal{L}=1-\cos(\hat z_t,\,z_t)=1-\cos\alpha$$
  • \(\alpha\) is the angle on the sphere between the guess \(\hat z_t\) and the true frame \(z_t\). The loss is 0 when they match and grows as they move apart.
  • For small misses it is almost exactly \(\alpha^2/2\) (\(\alpha\) in radians): 1° off costs 0.00015, 10° off costs 0.015, 20° off costs 0.060. So it behaves like a squared error measured along the sphere.
  • Each training step: pick 64 random (clip, frame) pairs from the 24 training clips, predict each frame from frame 0 and its time, average the loss, AdamW step, clip gradients at 1.0.
  • There is no separate term for distance or direction, no image loss and no smoothness term. Everything the model learns comes from this one score.
